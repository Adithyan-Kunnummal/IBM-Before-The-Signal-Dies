          How this works

          Susceptibility index. Seven measured features are scaled 0–1, averaged into hazard exposure (bushfire-prone area, mapped floodplain, recorded bushfires, cyclone tracks) and infrastructure sparsity (distance to the nearest Optus site, sites within 5 and 10 km), converted to WA percentiles and weighted 50/50. Top 20% High, next 30% Medium.

          Reported-outage model. XGBoost trained on the Optus significant-outage register (114 reported localities, 19 independent incidents) and tested on held-out incidents. It learns where significant outages get reported, which is mostly near Optus sites, so it complements the index rather than replacing it.

          Simulation. The ACCC data has no per-site footprints, so a locality counts as covered when a working Optus site sits within the assumed coverage radius of its representative point. Scenarios are illustrative, not reconstructions of real events.

          Live re-banding. When sites fail, each nearby locality's distance to the nearest working site and its working-site counts are recalculated with the index's own scaling. Hazard exposure is unchanged, so bands can only rise.

          Data. ABS 2021 Census, ACCC Optus sites (January 2025), DBCA fire history, DWER floodplain mapping, WA bushfire-prone areas, BOM cyclone tracks, Optus outage register.
